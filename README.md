## 🚀 Why This Repository Exists

Most technical learning follows this pattern:

```text
Learn a concept
      ↓
Watch a tutorial
      ↓
Copy an example
      ↓
Solve a predefined problem

But real engineering is different.

You are given a problem you have never seen before.

There is no tutorial.

There is no predefined algorithm.

There is no "correct approach" written at the top.

You have to figure it out.

That's what this repository is designed to train.

🎯 The Goal

I am working through 100 real-world-inspired case studies, starting from beginner-level
problems and gradually moving toward complex ML systems, distributed systems, product decisions,
and large-scale engineering problems.

The goal isn't to memorize 100 solutions.

The goal is to become better at:

Breaking down ambiguous problems
Asking the right questions
Making assumptions
Reasoning from first principles
Designing multiple solutions
Evaluating trade-offs
Thinking about failure modes
Designing scalable systems
Understanding where ML actually helps
Learning from mistakes
Extracting general principles
The ultimate test

Can I solve a problem I've never seen before?

🧩 Anatomy of a Case

Every case contains:

01 — Problem

What are we actually trying to solve?

02 — Objective

What does success mean?

03 — Constraints

What limits our solution?

04 — Assumptions

What are we assuming to be true?

05 — Data

What information do we have?

06 — Initial Solution

What would I build before researching the answer?

07 — Alternatives

What other approaches exist?

08 — Trade-offs

What do we gain and sacrifice?

09 — Decision

What would I choose and why?

10 — Architecture

How would the system work?

11 — Failure Modes

How can it break?

12 — Metrics

How do we know it works?

13 — Real-World Research

How do real systems approach this?

14 — What I Got Wrong

What did I misunderstand?

15 — General Principle

What lesson applies beyond this case?

🧪 Case Study Example
Case #01 — Netflix Recommendation System

Problem

Recommend movies that individual users are likely to watch.

My first thought

Use viewing history, genres, ratings and popularity.

What I didn't know

How to efficiently find relevant movies for millions of users.

After research

Discovered:

Candidate generation
Collaborative filtering
Content-based approaches
Ranking
Cold-start strategies
Offline vs online evaluation

What I got wrong

I initially assumed that ratings were the strongest signal.

General principle

A useful ML system is often a pipeline of multiple stages rather than a single model.

### One thing I'd change from most GitHub repositories


Don't make the README a giant wall of technical terminology.


Your **hook should be the journey**:


> **"I don't know the answer. Here's how I tried to figure it out. Here's where I was wrong.
Here's what the real world taught me."**


That makes the repository feel **alive**, rather than like another copied ML portfolio.


And if you eventually complete all 100, the most valuable section may actually be a **"My Evolution"**
page showing how your reasoning changed from Case #1 → #100. That's much more compelling to recruiters
than simply saying *"100 case studies completed."*
